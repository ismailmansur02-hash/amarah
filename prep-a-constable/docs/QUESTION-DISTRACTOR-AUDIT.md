# Question distractor audit — the "longest answer" tell

**Finding:** in 776 of 885 questions (88%) the correct answer is the longest option.
Correct options average 113 characters; wrong ones average 37 — the right answer is
over three times longer than the wrong ones.

A candidate who has read no law at all, picking only the longest option every time,
scores on average: **AP1 94%, AP2 76%, AP3 86%, AP4 86%** — a comfortable pass in every one.

This does not make any answer wrong. Every answer checked was legally correct. The problem
is that the wrong options are too short and too obviously wrong, so the app can be beaten
on shape rather than on law — and a recruit who scores 90% here may still walk into the
real assessment unprepared, because the real paper will not give that tell.

## What fixing it involves

Only Mr Mansur can write the content. The mechanical part is making the wrong options
match the right one in length and specificity — a plausible wrong answer should cite a
real but incorrect section, or state a rule that is nearly right, at the same level of
detail. The detail currently sitting only in the correct option (the "(DPP v Smith, 1960)"
style parentheticals, the capitalised key phrases) is itself part of the tell.
